If you own shares or mutual funds in India, you probably already receive a Consolidated Account Statement, or CAS, every month or every six months. Many investors never open it. In October 2026 it was in the news twice: the RBI said bank deposits will be added to it, and SEBI's chairman said insurance could follow. Here is what the CAS is, and why these changes matter.

## The short answer

A CAS is **one statement, linked to your PAN, that brings together the investments you hold across brokers, demat accounts and mutual fund houses**. Instead of checking five apps and three fund houses, you see everything in a single document: what you own, what it is worth, and what changed during the period.

## Who sends it, and when

The CAS is prepared by India's two depositories, **NSDL** and **CDSL**, using data that mutual fund houses (AMCs) and their registrars (such as CAMS and KFintech) send them. If you have demat accounts with both depositories, you still get one combined statement. Investors who hold mutual funds only, with no demat account, can get a mutual-fund CAS from the registrars.

SEBI tightened the timelines from **14 May 2025**:

| Statement | When you get it | Data deadline for AMCs and registrars | Email CAS sent by | Physical CAS sent by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Any month in which you had a transaction | 5th day of the next month | 12th day | 15th day |
| Half-yearly | April and October, if there were no transactions | 8th of April and October | 18th | 21st |

Since 2024, **email is the default** way of sending the CAS. A paper copy goes only to those who ask for it.

## What is inside

- Shares held in all your demat accounts, whichever broker you use
- Mutual fund units, both in demat form and in regular folios held directly with fund houses
- Government securities, corporate bonds and other instruments held in demat form
- Your **NPS** holdings, but only if you have given consent through the NPS record-keeping agency
- The value of your portfolio and how it changed during the period
- Transactions such as purchases, sales, SIP instalments, redemptions and switches
- Nomination status
- In half-yearly statements, the **commission your mutual fund distributor earned** and the expense ratio of each scheme

## What is not inside (yet)

- Bank savings accounts and fixed deposits
- Insurance policies
- Physical share certificates
- Small-savings schemes such as PPF and post-office deposits

## Why it is in the news

**Bank deposits are being added.** On **7 October 2026**, while announcing its bi-monthly monetary policy, the RBI said it is allowing SEBI-regulated depositories to include bank deposit information in the CAS. The data will flow through the RBI's **Account Aggregator** framework, with the customer's consent. The RBI expects this to be in place by **31 December 2026**. In the same announcement, it said Account Aggregators will become interoperable, so customers can reach data held by different providers through the aggregator of their choice.

**Insurance may come next.** On **15 October 2026**, at the annual conference of Sahamati (the industry body for Account Aggregators), SEBI chairman **Tuhin Kanta Pandey** said SEBI has written to the insurance regulator, IRDAI, about including insurance information in the CAS. This is at an exploratory stage. No timeline or final scope has been announced.

Put together, the direction is clear. The CAS is moving from a securities statement towards a **single view of a household's financial assets**: shares, bonds, mutual funds, pension, bank deposits and possibly insurance.

## Why it matters to you

1. **Catch mistakes or fraud early.** The CAS comes from the depository, not your broker. Any transaction you did not make, or a holding that has disappeared, shows up independently of your broker's app.
2. **See your real asset allocation.** When everything is in one place, it is easier to see how much is in equity, how much in debt and how much in cash. Bank deposits will make this picture complete.
3. **Check what you are paying.** The half-yearly CAS shows the commission your distributor earns. This often prompts investors to compare regular plans with lower-cost direct plans.
4. **Keep nominations in order.** The statement shows whether a nominee is registered for each holding.
5. **Help your family.** After an investor's death, legal heirs can use the CAS to find the securities and mutual funds held in that person's name. This avoids investments going unclaimed.

## How to get your CAS

1. **Check your email.** Search for "CAS", "NSDL" or "CDSL". The PDF is password-protected, and the email tells you what the password is.
2. **Update your contact details.** Make sure your email and PAN are correct with your broker or depository participant, and with your mutual fund folios.
3. **Request a statement.** The NSDL and CDSL websites let you request your CAS. For mutual funds, CAMS and KFintech provide a combined statement on request.
4. **Allow NPS, if you want it included.** Give consent through your NPS record-keeping agency.

## The takeaway

The CAS is one of the most useful documents a retail investor receives, and one of the most ignored. Open it once a month, check that every holding and transaction is yours, and look at the overall mix. Once bank deposits arrive, it will be the closest thing Indian investors have to a single balance sheet for their savings.
