A systematic investment plan (SIP) is simply a fixed amount invested every month. What makes it interesting is not the habit but the arithmetic: each instalment keeps earning returns for every month that follows it, and the returns themselves start earning returns.

## The formula

If you invest an amount $$P$$ at the start of every month for $$n$$ months, and your investment earns a monthly return of $$r$$, the value at the end is

$$
FV = P \times \frac{(1+r)^{n} - 1}{r} \times (1+r)
$$

For an expected yearly return of 12%, the monthly rate is $$r = 0.12 / 12 = 0.01$$. Twenty years is $$n = 240$$ months.

Plug in ₹10,000 a month and you get roughly **₹99.9 lakh**. You put in ₹24 lakh of your own money; the other ₹75.9 lakh is growth. You can try your own numbers in the calculator on the [home page]({{ '/' | relative_url }}).

## The ready reckoner

₹10,000 a month at an assumed 12% a year:

| Years | You invest | Value at the end | Value ÷ invested |
|------:|-----------:|-----------------:|-----------------:|
| 5     | ₹6.0 lakh  | ₹8.2 lakh        | 1.37×            |
| 10    | ₹12.0 lakh | ₹23.2 lakh       | 1.94×            |
| 15    | ₹18.0 lakh | ₹50.5 lakh       | 2.80×            |
| 20    | ₹24.0 lakh | ₹99.9 lakh       | 4.16×            |
| 25    | ₹30.0 lakh | ₹1.90 crore      | 6.33×            |
| 30    | ₹36.0 lakh | ₹3.53 crore      | 9.81×            |

Look at the last column. In the first ten years your money does not even double. Between year 20 and year 30 the value more than triples. Compounding is slow, then sudden, and most of the reward sits at the end.

## Time beats amount

Compare two investors who each put in exactly ₹24 lakh:

- **Early starter:** ₹10,000 a month for 20 years ends with about **₹99.9 lakh**.
- **Late starter:** ₹20,000 a month for 10 years ends with about **₹46.5 lakh**.

Same money in, less than half the result. The late starter simply gave compounding fewer years to work.

## Three things the brochures leave out

**1. Returns are not steady.** The formula assumes the same return every month. Real equity returns jump around, and the order of good and bad years matters, especially near the end when the pot is largest. Treat any single number as a scenario, not a promise. Here is how sensitive the 20-year result is to the assumed return:

| Assumed yearly return | Value after 20 years |
|----------------------:|---------------------:|
| 8%                    | ₹59.3 lakh           |
| 10%                   | ₹76.6 lakh           |
| 12%                   | ₹99.9 lakh           |
| 14%                   | ₹1.32 crore          |

**2. Inflation shrinks the prize.** If prices rise 6% a year, ₹99.9 lakh twenty years from now buys about what ₹31 lakh buys today. That is still a good outcome, but plan your goals in today's rupees.

**3. Costs and taxes come off the top.** Expense ratios, exit loads and capital-gains tax all reduce what you keep. Small differences in cost compound too, so a lower-cost fund is worth a careful look. Tax rules change often, so check the current ones before you redeem.

## A quick mental shortcut

The **rule of 72** says money doubles in roughly 72 ÷ (yearly return in %) years. At 12%, that is about every six years. It is approximate, but it is a handy check on any projection someone shows you.
